# Game Concept · Project Plateau

## One-page product definition

- **Selected direction:** *Proof Before Dark*
- **Product:** a free, single-player, first-person 3D connected-zone survival
adventure for desktop web
- **Session:** one complete 5–8 minute run
- **Target language and culture market:** English-first international players who
already understand first-person movement and readable survival/stealth cues,
but need no knowledge of Arthur Conan Doyle's novel
- **Player:** the expedition's forward scout
- **Promise:** cross a living prehistoric basin, earn credible proof through
dangerous observation, and bring it out rather than turning the ecosystem into
a shooting gallery

The selected slice translates the novel's proof lifecycle into a mature,
recognizable first-person loop:

```text
leave relative safety
  -> navigate by terrain and ecological signals
  -> observe and record a subject
  -> read the threat response
  -> evade or spend limited defense
  -> choose a return route
  -> extract with intact, credible proof or fail clearly
```

This is not an arena FPS, walking simulator, crafting sandbox, open-world
campaign or interactive chapter reader. Its differentiation is not a new verb
set. It applies familiar survival-exploration verbs to the novel's distinctive
question: **a sighting is not proof until the record survives the return**.

The largest unknown is whether recording and preserving evidence feels like a
physical first-person objective instead of a journal UI chore. The complete
prototype exists to answer that question before adding breadth.

## Experience pillars

| Pillar | Observable playtest evidence | Veto signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Read the living route** — water, tracks, calls, cover and silhouettes tell the player where to move and what may happen. | A first-time player reaches the observation site and later retraces a safe route by using world signals, without following a continuous objective line. | Testers move by trial-and-error wall hugging or a HUD arrow because the world itself is unreadable. |
| **Proof is an exposed action** — a useful record requires position, framing and time, then remains vulnerable while carried. | The player changes position or waits for a clearer behavior before recording, and can explain why one recovered record is stronger than another. | Recording is a single safe button press, or players cannot see any connection between framing/risk and the ending. |
| **Defense creates escape, not dominance** — cover, fire and a scarce loud tool alter pursuit without making wildlife targets to clear. | Players use terrain first, spend defense to recover a route, and continue toward extraction rather than turning back to hunt. | The reliable strategy is to kill or stun every creature before documenting at leisure. |

## Locked dimensions inherited from intake

All three candidate directions keep the same protected frame: English-first,
desktop web, real-time first-person 3D, connected zones, single player,
keyboard/mouse, Teen-equivalent peril, no backend, no monetization and one
complete 5–8 minute run. The player's performed fantasy remains scout →
document → survive → extract. Three.js/Vite remains the recorded implementation
candidate, not a concept differentiator.

The candidates differ only in unlocked choices: source slice, survival
subtype, dominant pressure, zone emphasis, evidence object, learning arc and
signature frame.

## Candidate A · Proof Before Dark — selected

- **Type:** first-person 3D survival adventure; ecological stealth-observation
slice
- **Same-play precedents:** *Subnautica*, *Alien: Isolation* and *The Long Dark*.
All three use the full recognizable backbone of first-person traversal,
environment/threat reading, objective acquisition, evasion or limited response,
route choice and arrival at relative safety.
- **Source slice:** the expedition's edge camp, brook/glade/rookery observations,
conditional cover and damaged-evidence return across Chapters 10–12 and 16.
- **One-line hook:** Photograph behavior no witness in London can dismiss, then
get the record out while the ecosystem reacts to your intrusion.
- **Identity / fantasy:** an expedition forward scout who becomes credible by
reading and surviving a living world, not by conquering it.

- **Core verbs:** traverse, listen/look, inspect traces, frame/record, use cover,
evade, spend a limited defensive tool, choose a route, extract.
- **Loop:** leave the thorn camp → follow ecological signals → improve a sighting
into evidence → trigger/read a behavior change → use terrain or defense to
recover a route → return with proof.
- **Pressure:** better evidence demands exposure and time; the return route may no
longer be safe; loud defense can solve the immediate distance problem while
creating a wider risk.
- **Mastery difference:** a skilled player recognizes behavior and cover early,
captures stronger evidence from a safer angle, preserves defense and returns by
a route that remains viable. A novice treats the subject as scenery, records too
soon or too close, and improvises after detection.

**Three-part action arc**

1. **Explore:** traverse from relative safety and inspect water, tracks and
   calls; only the edge route and indirect evidence are accessible.
2. **Develop:** reach an observation position and begin the new exposed action
   of framing/recording a living subject; behavior quality changes proof value.
3. **Mature:** after the world reacts, combine threat reading, cover, limited
   defense and a return-route choice while physically preserving the record;
   the extraction state becomes reachable.

- **Source-specific rule conversion:** the reusable `scan/record` verb from
*Subnautica* acts on evidence that can remain doubtful, become credible through
behavior, or be damaged in transit. The cost is exposure and a changed route;
the world response is animal awareness and a proof-dependent extraction result.
Chapters 4, 10–12 and 16 establish every link.

- **Closest same-play precedent:** *Subnautica*. The verb set stays recognizable;
only its object, cost and world response change—from acquiring durable scan
knowledge in an alien ocean to creating fragile proof in a disputed 1912 field
expedition. Crafting, bases and vehicles are not part of the slice.

- **Signature playable frame:** the player lowers a brass-and-leather field
camera as a pterodactyl shadow crosses an iguanodon glade; the red cliff and
blue-clay rookery read beyond the trees, while a lit defensive flare occupies
the other hand. The frame shows identity, recorded subject, cover, threat and
the escape tool at once.

- **Minimum slice:** the entire promised run—leave Fort Challenger, read a short
connected route, obtain one behavior-grade record, survive one ecological
response, choose a return path and reach extraction with a clear proof result.
It proves the proof lifecycle, terrain counterplay and restart loop. It omits
the Amazon approach, crafting, dialogue, faction war, full lake, free-roaming
open world, multiple weapons and a campaign.
- **Largest risk:** photographic evidence feels like filling a UI bar rather than
a dangerous physical act.
- **Minimum falsification test:** can a player capture one visibly better record
by changing position/timing, then make a different route or defense decision
because the act changed the threat state?

## Candidate B · Fire Across the Lake

- **Type:** first-person 3D survival adventure; nocturnal reconnaissance/pursuit
slice
- **Same-play precedents:** *Alien: Isolation* and *The Long Dark*. Both use
first-person navigation from relative safety, searching/observing for objective
information, reading an underpowered survival threat, evasion or a scarce tool,
and reaching shelter/next objective rather than clearing enemies.
- **Source slice:** Malone's solo journey from the camp down the brook to the
central lake, cave fires, scent-to-sight pursuit and concealed trap in Chapter
12.
- **One-line hook:** Follow running water to prove people live across a moonlit
prehistoric lake, then break a predator's trail before it sees you.
- **Identity / fantasy:** the forward scout as solitary night route-reader.

- **Core verbs:** traverse, listen, inspect, record, mark route, hide, break trail,
use fire, sprint and return.
- **Loop:** follow a sound landmark → inspect and record distant life → notice
pursuit → distinguish scent tracking from visual chase → break contact through
terrain/fire → relocate the brook and return.
- **Pressure:** darkness withholds sight while sound helps both navigation and
threat awareness; staying at the lake improves evidence but consumes the safe
return window.
- **Mastery difference:** a skilled player preserves orientation, notices the
tracking-mode change and breaks line of sight before sprinting. A novice outruns
the route, reveals themselves and loses the brook.

**Three-part action arc**

1. **Explore:** navigate by brook sound and landmarks through dark forest.
2. **Develop:** reach the lake edge and gain the record/route-marking actions,
   exposing the opposite cave fires and aquatic silhouettes.
3. **Mature:** when pursuit begins, read scent versus sight, open a different
   refuge path and combine concealment, fire and sprinting to return.

- **Source-specific rule conversion:** the repeated `read/evade` verbs from
*Alien: Isolation* and *The Long Dark* operate on a predator that changes from
scent tracking to sight after visual contact. The player changes that state
through route and concealment, not a scripted escape (Chapter 12).

- **Closest same-play precedent:** *Alien: Isolation*. The known stealth-pursuit
grammar is transferred to open jungle navigation and source-specific sensory
rules; no franchise creature, tracker UI or station structure is borrowed.

- **Signature playable frame:** silver lake water and distant red cave fires fill
the opening beyond black reeds; in the foreground a field note is half-finished
while a huge three-toed reflection enters the water behind the player.

- **Minimum slice:** camp → brook route → lake observation → one tracking-state
change → return/fail. It proves whether audio-led navigation and distinct threat
perception remain legible at web-demo length. It omits the rookery, multiple
proof subjects, factions, combat and the final cave escape.
- **Largest risk:** low light and pursuit make the project read as generic horror,
hiding the colorful 3D expedition-world promise.
- **Minimum falsification test:** can a new player state whether the predator is
following scent or sight and deliberately break the correct signal without a
text tutorial?

## Candidate C · The Eighteenth Cave

- **Type:** first-person 3D survival adventure; route-extraction/survey slice
- **Same-play precedents:** *The Long Dark* and *Subnautica*. Both ask players to
leave safety, navigate and map connected natural spaces, gather information or
resources under environmental/wildlife risk, use learned landmarks/tools, and
return or push toward a safer goal.
- **Source slice:** the far escarpment, eighteen-opening chart, misleading dead
end, missed fork, moonlit exit and rope descent of Chapter 15, with the fragile
proof burden from Chapters 12 and 16.
- **One-line hook:** Decode a local cave survey, find the branch your torchlight
made you miss, and descend with the expedition's surviving proof.
- **Identity / fantasy:** the forward scout as exit surveyor and proof carrier.

- **Core verbs:** survey, compare landmarks to a chart, traverse, mark a branch,
manage light, carry/leave evidence, verify an exit and extract.
- **Loop:** inspect the cave row → translate map relation into a route → enter and
test a passage → recognize the dead end and missed fork → choose what proof can
be carried → verify and descend through the moonlit rift.
- **Pressure:** limited visibility creates navigation errors; carrying proof makes
route commitment and recovery more consequential; local observers may close
the departure window.
- **Mastery difference:** a skilled player treats the chart as spatial relation,
marks the unlit fork and verifies the rope route before committing cargo. A
novice reads it as a waypoint list and mistakes the lit dead end for failure.

**Three-part action arc**

1. **Explore:** survey the external cave row and align the chart to visible
   openings.
2. **Develop:** gain torch/route marking inside the selected cave and discover
   that light reveals surfaces while hiding the earlier branch.
3. **Mature:** verify the new exit, choose portable proof and perform the new
   rope extraction through the reachable rift.

- **Source-specific rule conversion:** the mature `map/traverse` verbs from *The
Long Dark* act on Maretas's relational cave chart; the cost is that the first
obvious reading produces a dead end, and success requires respecting local
knowledge and checking the route's physical branch (Chapter 15).

- **Closest same-play precedent:** *The Long Dark*. Familiar landmark navigation
is applied to a source-specific cave diagram and proof burden; its wilderness
simulation, meters and broad sandbox are excluded.

- **Signature playable frame:** charcoal animal drawings and expedition shadow
curve along gray cave walls while a round plane of red-white moonlight reveals
the exit; a strapped specimen case and hanging rope make extraction readable.

- **Minimum slice:** survey the cave row → enter one mapped cave → encounter the
false end → recover the branch → choose proof → descend. It proves route
interpretation and physical evidence burden. It omits a full outdoor ecology,
active creature pursuit, combat, factions and the approach journey.
- **Largest risk:** the strongest action becomes cave-navigation puzzle solving,
so the project may still look like a literary puzzle experience rather than a
visually undeniable 3D survival game.
- **Minimum falsification test:** after seeing the external cave row and chart,
can a player recover the missed fork through spatial reasoning rather than
guessing or reading an explanation?

## Hard veto check

| Direction | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A · Proof Before Dark | **PASS** | Repeated movement/observation/evasion/extraction actions have state consequences; proof loss enacts the source's central tension; three high-adoption same-play precedents exist; the complete slice is bounded. |
| B · Fire Across the Lake | **PASS** | Its sensory pursuit rule changes repeatable navigation and evasion; two high-adoption same-play precedents exist; no fixed dialogue or production-scale world is required. |
| C · The Eighteenth Cave | **PASS** | Survey, marking, traversal and cargo decisions change reachable space; the local chart drives the source-specific rule; two high-adoption same-play precedents exist. |

No direction depends on reading dialogue, a fixed click-through plot, a service,
a production-scale open world, modern adaptation assets or a novel verb set.
All three action arcs add a reachable space or usable action rather than only
raising a counter.

## Comparison and selection

| Dimension | A · Proof Before Dark | B · Fire Across the Lake | C · The Eighteenth Cave |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source-rule fit | Strongest complete proof lifecycle plus conditional ecology. | Strongest single threat-perception rule. | Strongest map/exit causality and local-knowledge handoff. |
| Source emotion enacted | Wonder becomes responsibility: the player must risk and preserve a credible record. | Solitary awe becomes fear and disciplined retreat. | Entrapment becomes careful trust in a route authored by someone else. |
| Player agency | Multiple linked choices in evidence quality, exposure, cover, defense and return path. | Clear pursuit decisions, but fewer meaningful proof outcomes. | Strong route decisions, lighter creature interaction. |
| Visual communication | Daylight prehistoric subject + camera + threat + red cliff communicates 3D game and source at once. | Beautiful silhouette, but darkness risks generic-horror screenshots. | Distinct cave/exit frame, but could be mistaken for an environmental puzzle demo. |
| Cultural handling | Avoids conquest and makes non-lethal observation the success condition. | Avoids faction material almost entirely. | Can credit Maretas's knowledge, but needs careful context to avoid reducing it to a puzzle prop. |
| Completion risk | Highest creature/recording interaction load, still bounded to one route and behavior response. | Lowest art breadth, but audio/AI legibility must carry most of the experience. | Lowest fauna load, but may not overturn the perception that the toolkit produces text-forward games. |

**Selection: `selected — A · Proof Before Dark`.** In quick mode, it has the
strongest evidence because one bounded run can prove all four locked fantasy
verbs, produce a shareable daylight 3D frame and enact the novel's opening-to-
ending proof tension through play. Its largest risk is also testable without a
campaign: if evidence quality and preservation do not change how the player
moves, the direction should be abandoned rather than decorated.

Direction B remains the fallback encounter model if the chosen threat cannot be
made readable in daylight. Direction C remains a possible later extraction
beat, but it must not displace the selected proof-and-ecology loop.

## Non-negotiable experience promises

- The player earns proof through observation under exposure, not by touching a
  collectible or finishing a cutscene.
- The living world communicates route and danger before the HUD explains them.
- A creature response changes the player's return problem after recording.
- Defensive action restores a chance to escape; it does not convert wildlife
  into a clearable enemy roster.
- Reaching safety without credible, intact proof is visibly different from a
  complete success.
- The source's colonial conquest and racial hierarchy are not presented as
  neutral lore, progression or spectacle.

## Minimum validation question

**Can a first-time player move from cover into a better observation position,
create a visibly stronger photographic record, recognize that the act changed
the wildlife threat, and alter the return route or defensive choice so the
record reaches safety intact?**

The smallest valid test is one connected route with one living proof subject,
one behavior-state change, at least two usable return responses and an ending
that distinguishes proof quality and survival. A build that tests only camera
framing, only movement or only a chase does not answer the question.

Abandon or return to concept selection if testers cannot connect their framing
choice to proof quality, cannot read the changed threat state from the world,
or treat shooting the subject as the safest completion strategy.

## Open questions for GAME_DESIGN

- Which single living subject best exposes both wonder and evidence quality
  without requiring a production-scale animation set?
- Which threat response can be read in daylight through motion, sound and
  terrain rather than a detection meter?
- How is a weak, credible or damaged record shown without making the player read
  a dense journal during the run?
- What two return responses are meaningfully different but share the same
  compact connected-zone layout?
- What does the limited defensive tool interrupt, what new risk does it create,
  and how does the player learn that from feedback?
- How can Maretas's or Zambo's contribution remain visible if a route clue or
  extraction preparation enters the selected slice?
- Which evidence result is authoritative for the prototype's restart/ending
  checks while leaving subjective “fun” and audience belief to human judgment?
