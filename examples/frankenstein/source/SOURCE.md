## Which edition this is, and how that was established

This is the **1831 revised edition**, not the 1818 first edition. The Gutenberg file
carries neither Percy Shelley's 1818 Preface nor Mary Shelley's 1831 Introduction, so
the edition cannot be read off the front matter. Two structural and textual markers
settle it:

1. **Chapter arrangement.** 4 Letters followed by 24 continuous chapters, with no
   volume divisions. The 1818 text is three volumes (7 + 9 + 7 chapters) whose numbering
   restarts each volume; 1831 renumbers straight through to 24.
2. **Elizabeth Lavenza's parentage.** In 1818 she is Victor's first cousin, his father's
   sister's daughter. Here she is an orphaned Milanese child whose father was one of the
   *schiavi ognor frementi*, taken in by Victor's mother and called "my more than
   sister" — the 1831 revision, which removed the cousin relation.

The verification matters downstream: the two editions differ on how much Victor chooses
versus is fated to, which is exactly the axis this adaptation reads for gameable
material.

## Completeness

Chapter headings are the bare line `Chapter N` (arabic, no chapter titles), 1 through 24,
in order. The four framing letters (`Letter 1`–`Letter 4`) are Walton's, and are the
outer frame rather than numbered chapters; the structure declared in `example.json`
counts the 24 chapters and the coverage table accounts for the letters in prose.

## Licence boundary

Mary Shelley died 1 February 1851, so the novel is in the public domain in the United
States (published 1818/1831, long pre-1930) and in the United Kingdom and EU (life + 70,
clear since 1922). There is no estate or foundation asserting rights in the text.

Two constraints follow, and they bind the art direction rather than the text:

- **The creature's famous look is not from this book.** The flat head, neck electrodes
  and green pallor are Universal's 1931 make-up design, which is separately
  copyrighted. This adaptation takes its creature from Shelley's own description —
  yellow translucent skin drawn tight over working muscle and vessels, lustrous black
  hair, watery eyes, straight black lips, eight feet tall.
- **Recent screen adaptations are off-limits as reference**, including the 2025 Netflix
  film, whose creature design is its own protected work even where it also claims
  fidelity to the novel. Period sources for anatomy theatres, Ingolstadt lecture halls
  and Arctic whaling are public-domain research material; contemporary film, television,
  game and animation frames are not.
